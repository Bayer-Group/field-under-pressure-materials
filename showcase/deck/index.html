06Future

What else metagenomics can tell us

Early disease alarmFinds pathogens nobody tested for
Soil trends over yearsSpot threats years in advance
Proof for biocontrolCheck biopesticides really take hold

Suppressive soil forecastPredict if outbreaks take hold
Beyond soil
Livestock healthTrack antibiotic resistance in herds
All six cards come in together on entering the slide. 1 Early disease alarm: with climate change, diseases spread faster than anyone expects. Shotgun metagenomics doesn't look for anything specific, so it can find, say, a corn-killing fungus from Lebanon in a Polish field before anyone thought to test for it. 2 Soil trends: sample the same plot over the years. If a harmful bacterium shows up at a tiny share, we can predict it will be a problem in about five years, not now. If a beneficial one is fading, we know to help it. 3 Biocontrol: monitor how biological pesticides and other biological plant protection and support actually work in the soil, instead of traditional chemical ones. 4 Spray per pathogen: targeted spraying chosen by the specific pathogen present, not only by its location on the field. 5 Suppressive soil: sequencing shows whether beneficial microbes around the roots carry antimicrobial genes that naturally block pathogens, so we can predict whether an outbreak will actually take hold. 6 Livestock health (beyond soil): the same method works for livestock, not only soil. Routine metagenomic monitoring of manure, irrigation runoff and post-harvest produce maps the spread of antibiotic resistance genes (the "resistome") and foodborne pathogens before they enter the human food supply.
